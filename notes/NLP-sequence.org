What the NLP-bearing stages of the gfmrag pipeline actually do, and — crucially
— how each one scales with corpus size. The headline: *per-kilobyte cost is not
constant*, because the stages have fundamentally different scaling laws (fixed,
linear, super-linear). Treating the pipeline as "X seconds per KB" is the wrong
model and hides where time actually goes.

* The stages

** 1. NER + OpenIE  (≈25% of NLP, ~52 s) — scales LINEARLY
Two LLM calls per document against vLLM (OpenAI-compatible):
- *NER* extracts the named entities (companies, ABNs, places, dates, sectors).
- *OpenIE* turns the passage + entity list into RDF triples
  =[subject, relation, object]=, guided to valid JSON.
This is the only stage whose work is genuinely proportional to text volume.
- *Scaling:* ~linear in corpus KB. Measured ~5.7 s/KB at shard-0 (warm), but
  the rate *falls* on larger shards as vLLM's continuous batching saturates —
  so 5.7 s/KB is an upper bound at the smallest scale, not a constant.
- *Note:* the first document of a cold run absorbs the vLLM cold start
  (minutes); excluded here as infra. Output quality is governed by the prompt
  profile (see fact gating), not timing.

** 2. ColBERT entity-linking  (≈70% of NLP, ~144 s) — MIXED
Resolves surface variants of entity/phrase nodes to canonical nodes, building
a PLAID index over the node names. Three sub-parts with *different* scaling:

*** 2a. CUDA JIT compile  (~84 s, ≈40% of the whole NLP sequence) — FIXED
pylate/ColBERT JIT-compiles CUDA C++ extensions (=decompress_residuals_cpp=,
=packbits_cpp=) at first use — the reason the image needs the CUDA *devel*
base. This is a flat ~84 s *regardless of corpus size*. At shard-0 it is the
single largest NLP cost; at shard-3 it is the same 84 s against far more real
work, so it shrinks toward irrelevance. *Per-instance ephemeral* — paid on
every cold run, not cached between executions.

*** 2b. PLAID index build  (~58 s, ≈28%) — SUPER-LINEAR
Encodes node embeddings and builds the PLAID/IVF index structure. Grows faster
than linearly with the number of distinct nodes, so its share *increases* with
corpus size — the opposite of the JIT compile.

*** 2c. Document encode  (~2 s, ≈1%) — linear
First GPU embedding pass over the documents. Cheap.

** 3. Synonym edges + graph write  (≈5%, ~11 s) — SUPER-LINEAR
Adds cosine-similarity "synonym" edges between node names
(=BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5=, threshold 0.8), then writes
=nodes.csv / edges.csv / relations.csv=. The similarity step is pairwise over
the node set (≈ n²), so like the index build its cost share grows with corpus
size.

** 4. Stage-2 embedding build  (QA-side, not cleanly isolated) — linear
Builds the retrieval index the GFM reasons over: node/relation embeddings under
the checkpoint's embedder (34M ⇒ Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B / 1024-dim), persisted to
the bucket. Happens at QA startup, bundled inside the QA cold start, so a clean
isolated time was not captured. Small at tested scales (seconds for shard-0's
126 nodes); scales ~linearly with node count.

* Scaling summary

| Stage                       | % of NLP (shard-0) | scaling law      | share as corpus grows |
|-----------------------------+--------------------+------------------+-----------------------|
| NER + OpenIE                | 25%                | linear           | ~flat (batching helps)|
| ColBERT JIT compile         | 40%                | FIXED            | shrinks → negligible  |
| PLAID index build           | 28%                | super-linear     | grows                 |
| doc encode                  | 1%                 | linear           | ~flat                 |
| synonym edges + write       | 5%                 | super-linear (n²)| grows                 |
| stage-2 embedding (QA-side) | (not isolated)     | linear           | ~flat                 |

* Why "per kilobyte" is the wrong question

At small scale the NLP time is dominated by the *fixed* JIT compile (40%); as
the corpus grows, OpenIE's linear term and the index/synonym super-linear terms
take over while the JIT amortizes to nothing. So a single "seconds per KB" for
the whole pipeline is meaningless — it would be huge at 9 KB (fixed cost spread
over little text) and misleading at 700 KB (super-linear terms dominating).

The only stage with a defensible per-KB number is *LLM extraction (NER+OpenIE):
~3–6 s/KB warm, trending down with scale.* Everything else is governed by node
count and fixed setup, not text volume. This is exactly why the cost ladder
measures *wall-clock per rung* rather than per-byte, and why the operational
rule is "pack shards as large as the 1-hour task cap allows" — you are mostly
paying to get the container ready (image pull + JIT), not to process bytes.

* Caveat on precision

These percentages are reconstructed from one shard-0 run's logs and are rough.
A precise per-stage profile at a large shard (where fixed costs are amortized
and the super-linear terms are visible) needs explicit phase timers added to the
extractor; the shape above is directionally correct but the exact split shifts
with scale.
